model one

Winter Chill & Almond Yield

Testing whether winter chill accumulation can serve as an early indicator of almond yield per acre in Fresno County, using 21 years of NOAA weather and USDA agricultural data.

python ols regression pandas data cleaning
Team Project

Findings

Fresno bearing acres vs. almond yield per acre (year-to-year variation), 2002-2022. This divergence is why yield per acre, not acreage, became the modeling target.

Winter chill hours vs. almond yield per acre, with a fitted trend line - a moderate positive relationship (r = 0.43), in the direction the hypothesis predicted.

Correlation matrix of winter weather features and yield shows how tangled the individual predictors are with each other, which makes their combined model coefficients unstable.

Overview

problem

Winter temperatures in California's Central Valley are trending warmer, and almond trees need enough winter cold (chill) during dormancy to bloom evenly. The team wanted to know whether a measurable winter signal temperature or accumulated chill could serve as an early, observable indicator of relative almond yield in Fresno County before harvest.

approach

Combined 21 years (2002-2022) of hourly NOAA temperature data with USDA yield-per-acre figures, engineering winter features like average, minimum, and maximum temperature, temperature variability, freeze hours, and chill hours (32-45F). Ran single feature OLS regressions for each winter feature, then a combined multiple regression across all six, plus a correlation matrix to check how the features related to each other and to yield.

results

Winter conditions did move with yield: maximum winter temperature was the strongest single predictor (R2 = 0.29), and chill hours pointed in the hypothesized direction with a moderate positive correlation (r = 0.43). The combined six-feature model explained a meaningful share of year-to-year yield variation (adjusted R2 = 0.59, p = 0.003) a real signal, but not clean or strong enough on its own to serve as a precise forecast.

next steps

Future iterations would pull temperature from multiple stations across the county instead of one, replace the simplified chill hours count with a validated chill portions model, and add drivers the current model doesn't capture irrigation, orchard age, and variety to see whether the pattern holds county to county.
